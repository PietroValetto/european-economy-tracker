European Economy Tracker

About

A running read on the euro area economy, in charts you can check yourself.

What this is

The European Economy Tracker puts the numbers that decide your prices, your pay and your borrowing costs in one place: growth, jobs, wages, inflation, interest rates, public finances and the indicators only Europe has. Each chart shows the latest reading, where it came from and what changed.

Who makes it

It is made by Pietro Valetto as part of Popular Economics, a newsletter that explains the economic forces behind your money. If you want the story behind the charts, that is where it gets told.

Where the data come from

Every number comes from a public, official source:

  • Eurostat, the EU's statistical office
  • the European Central Bank
  • the European Commission
  • the OECD, the Bank for International Settlements and the IMF
  • Indeed Hiring Lab, for job postings
  • national statistical institutes

Each chart names its source and links to it. The methods page explains how every series is built, including the few measures the tracker calculates itself, which carry an "Original" tag.

How it is built

The site rebuilds itself automatically every morning and again on the mornings important figures come out. Nobody checks each chart by hand before it goes live, so it may contain errors. If you spot one, tell us through the newsletter.

Where the idea came from

The tracker was inspired by Econvitals, Jason Furman's dashboard of the US economy. This is the same idea for Europe, built on European data and written for people who are not economists.
